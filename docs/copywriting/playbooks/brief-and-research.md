# Copy Brief And Research Playbook

## Purpose

Turn business facts, customer evidence, and channel constraints into a reliable drafting contract. This step prevents fluent copy from solving the wrong problem or inventing support for an incomplete offer.

## Required Inputs

Do not begin customer-ready copy until these inputs are known or explicitly marked Unknown:

- business objective and success measure;
- audience and buying role;
- funnel stage and current awareness;
- problem, desired outcome, and current alternative;
- offer, scope, price, terms, delivery, and support;
- differentiated method or mechanism;
- verified proof and material limitations;
- objections and decision criteria;
- primary call to action;
- channel, format, placement, and length constraints;
- approved brand voice and prohibited language;
- claim, privacy, legal, platform, and approval constraints; and
- owner of final publication.

Use the [Copy Brief Template](../templates/copy-brief.md) as the input contract.

## Evidence Ledger

Maintain a compact ledger before drafting:

Use the canonical evidence states `Verified`, `Qualified`, `Illustrative`, `Unknown`, and `Prohibited`. `Illustrative` means clearly synthetic or hypothetical material for an internal mockup; it must never appear as a real customer result or customer-ready claim.

| Proposed statement | Evidence source | State | Approved wording or limit | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product capability | Current product or technical source | Verified, Qualified, Unknown, or Prohibited | Exact supported scope | Product owner |
| Customer outcome | Approved case evidence | Verified, Qualified, Illustrative, Unknown, or Prohibited | Population, period, and conditions | Customer owner |
| Price or term | Authoritative commercial system | Verified, Unknown, or Prohibited | Current amount, term, and effective date | Commercial owner |
| Testimonial or logo | Consent and source record | Verified, Unknown, or Prohibited | Approved quotation and attribution | Brand owner |
| Deadline or capacity | Current operational state | Verified, Unknown, or Prohibited | Exact date, quantity, or condition | Offer owner |

If a claim has no row, it has no publication basis.

## Audience Research

Capture the audience in operational terms:

- **Situation:** What is happening when the need becomes urgent?
- **Job:** What progress is the buyer trying to make?
- **Friction:** What costs time, money, attention, confidence, or political capital?
- **Desired outcome:** What becomes easier, faster, safer, clearer, or more profitable?
- **Current alternative:** What do they use, tolerate, postpone, or build manually?
- **Decision criteria:** What must be true before they will act?
- **Objections:** What could make the decision feel risky, unnecessary, premature, or difficult?
- **Language:** Which customer terms are accurate and approved? Which internal terms create confusion?
- **Buying role:** User, operator, champion, budget owner, technical reviewer, legal reviewer, or executive sponsor?

Do not reduce an audience to demographics when the buying situation, desired progress, constraints, and authority are more predictive.

## Awareness And Readiness

Choose the opening based on what the reader already knows:

| Reader state | What the copy should do first |
|---|---|
| Does not recognize the problem | Make the situation recognizable without manufacturing fear |
| Recognizes the problem | Clarify cost, cause, and priority |
| Knows solution categories | Explain the differentiated method and tradeoffs |
| Knows the product | Prove fit, remove objections, and make the offer concrete |
| Ready to act | Reduce friction and state the next step clearly |

Do not explain a category to a ready buyer for ten paragraphs. Do not ask an unaware reader to buy before the problem and relevance are clear.

## Market Sophistication

Estimate how familiar the audience is with competing promises:

- **Low familiarity:** a direct, supportable promise may be enough;
- **Growing familiarity:** add specificity, audience fit, and a clear reason to believe;
- **Crowded market:** lead with a differentiated mechanism, proof, tradeoff, or point of view;
- **Skeptical market:** narrow the promise, expose limitations, and make verification easy;
- **Commodity market:** improve the offer, delivery, risk, speed, integration, or service model instead of relying on louder wording.

When every competitor uses the same claim, the answer is usually stronger substance or sharper positioning, not another adjective.

## Voice Definition

Define voice with observable behaviors:

| Dimension | Choice | Writing implication |
|---|---|---|
| Directness | concise to explanatory | Sentence length and amount of setup |
| Formality | conversational to formal | Contractions, vocabulary, and structure |
| Energy | calm to urgent | Pace and emphasis, without invented urgency |
| Expertise | accessible to technical | Required definitions and depth |
| Confidence | cautious to assertive | Claim scope must remain evidence-matched |
| Warmth | neutral to personal | Reader acknowledgment and relational language |

Also record three positive examples of desired behavior and three prohibited tendencies. Avoid vague instructions such as make it punchy or sound premium without defining what those mean.

## Research Synthesis

Convert research into decisions, not a data dump:

1. list repeated customer situations and desired outcomes;
2. group objections by risk, effort, trust, timing, authority, and alternatives;
3. identify the strongest verified proof for each material claim;
4. distinguish customer language from internal terminology;
5. rank angles by relevance, differentiation, evidence, and offer fit;
6. state the primary message and what the copy will deliberately omit; and
7. record Unknowns that block customer-ready drafting.

## Angle Selection

Score candidate angles from 0 to 2 on each dimension:

- urgent relevance to the audience;
- differentiated insight or mechanism;
- strength of available proof;
- fit with the actual offer;
- fit with the channel and funnel stage;
- brand credibility; and
- compliance and operational risk.

Choose the strongest strategic angle. Do not combine several weak angles into one unfocused draft.

## Handoff Contract

A drafting handoff should contain:

- one-sentence objective;
- one primary audience and buying situation;
- one primary promise or desired outcome;
- one mechanism or reason to believe;
- approved proof;
- offer and terms;
- primary objection set;
- one call to action;
- channel constraints;
- voice rules;
- claim ledger; and
- unresolved Unknowns.

The handoff is complete only when another writer can explain what must be true, what may be said, what must not be said, and what action the reader should take.

## Validation

- Every material claim maps to evidence.
- The offer matches current operational reality.
- The audience is defined by situation and decision role.
- The selected angle fits awareness, sophistication, channel, and proof.
- Voice is described through observable writing behavior.
- Unknowns are visible and do not leak into customer-ready copy as facts.
