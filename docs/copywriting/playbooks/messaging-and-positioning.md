# Messaging And Positioning Playbook

## Purpose

Define what the audience should understand, believe, remember, and do before polishing individual headlines or sentences.

## Positioning Core

Complete this statement internally:

> For a specific audience in a specific situation, the offer helps create a desired outcome through a differentiated method, unlike the current alternative, with a credible reason to believe.

This is a decision tool, not necessarily published copy. If the sentence needs several audiences, unrelated outcomes, or vague category language, the positioning is not yet focused.

## Message Hierarchy

Prioritize messages in this order:

1. **Relevance:** This is for someone like me in a situation I recognize.
2. **Outcome:** I understand the useful change being offered.
3. **Mechanism:** I understand how or why the change can happen.
4. **Differentiation:** I understand why this approach is meaningfully different.
5. **Proof:** I have a credible reason to believe it.
6. **Offer:** I understand what I receive, what it requires, and what it costs.
7. **Action:** I know the next step and what happens afterward.

A page may contain more information, but it should not force the reader to discover this hierarchy by accident.

## Problem Definition

A useful problem statement names:

- the observable situation;
- the practical consequence;
- why the current approach fails or creates friction;
- who is affected; and
- why the issue matters now.

Avoid inflated pain language. The objective is recognition and priority, not emotional manipulation.

## Promise Design

A credible promise combines:

- a desired outcome;
- a defined audience or situation;
- a realistic scope;
- a relevant constraint or tradeoff; and
- a reason to believe.

Narrow promises often convert better than broad promises because the reader can evaluate them. Replace universal transformation language with the most valuable outcome the evidence can support.

## Mechanism

The mechanism explains why the outcome is plausible. It may be:

- a workflow;
- a technical integration;
- a sequence of decisions;
- a service model;
- a product capability;
- a diagnostic method;
- a data advantage; or
- a reduction in steps, delays, handoffs, or failure modes.

Name a mechanism only when it is real and understandable. A branded label without substance is not differentiation.

## Features, Benefits, And Consequences

Translate a feature through three levels:

1. **Capability:** What the product or service does.
2. **Operational effect:** What becomes easier, faster, safer, or more consistent.
3. **Business or personal consequence:** Why that effect matters to this audience.

Stop before inventing an outcome. A feature can support a benefit without proving a specific financial or performance result.

## Big-Idea Test

A strong central idea should be:

- relevant to the audience's current situation;
- simple enough to repeat accurately;
- differentiated enough to earn attention;
- expandable across the copy;
- compatible with the offer; and
- supportable with evidence.

Reject an idea that is merely surprising, dramatic, or clever but cannot organize the message and proof.

## Headline And Hook Decisions

Choose a headline job before choosing wording:

- identify the audience;
- name a desired outcome;
- expose a costly problem;
- introduce a differentiated mechanism;
- lead with verified proof;
- challenge a familiar but weak assumption;
- make a useful comparison;
- offer a diagnostic or checklist; or
- announce a concrete offer or change.

A hook earns the next unit of attention. It does not need to summarize the entire page. It must, however, deliver on the expectation it creates.

## Headline Quality Gate

Review each headline for:

- audience relevance;
- clarity without supporting context;
- specificity supported by facts;
- a meaningful benefit or insight;
- consistency with the body and offer;
- brand fit;
- absence of fabricated certainty, urgency, or proof; and
- distinctiveness without source imitation.

Generate alternatives by changing the strategic angle: audience, problem, outcome, mechanism, proof, contrast, or offer. Changing only adjectives is not meaningful variation.

## Lead Types

Select a lead based on awareness and proof:

| Lead type | Best use | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Direct outcome | Reader knows the problem and values the result | Broad or unsupported promise |
| Problem recognition | Reader feels friction but has not prioritized it | Manufactured fear or excessive agitation |
| Mechanism | Market is crowded with similar claims | Branded jargon without explanatory value |
| Proof-led | Strong evidence is available and relevant | Proof without context or consent |
| Contrarian reframe | Familiar assumption blocks action | Empty provocation or false dichotomy |
| Story-led | A real, approved narrative makes the lesson concrete | Slow setup, invented details, or unclear relevance |
| Diagnostic | Reader wants to assess fit or risk | Fake precision or a disguised pitch |

## Specificity And Contrast

Useful specificity includes exact audience, situation, workflow, deliverable, condition, limitation, time frame, or evidence. Numbers are not automatically specific if they are irrelevant or unsupported.

Contrast may compare:

- before and after states;
- old and new processes;
- manual and automated effort;
- fragmented and integrated systems;
- visible symptom and underlying cause;
- generic category and specialized fit; or
- high-friction next step and low-friction next step.

Use fair comparisons. Do not misrepresent alternatives or competitors.

## Message Map

For each major section, record:

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Reader question | What must be answered here? |
| Section claim | What is the single takeaway? |
| Evidence | What supports it? |
| Objection | What doubt must be resolved? |
| Transition | Why should the reader continue? |
| Action | Is a call to action appropriate here? |

This prevents repeated claims, orphaned proof, and sections that exist only because a template included them.

## Validation

- One audience and one primary outcome are clear.
- The mechanism is real, explainable, and differentiated.
- Benefits do not outrun capabilities or evidence.
- The opening matches awareness and sophistication.
- Headlines vary by strategic angle rather than cosmetic wording.
- Proof appears near the claim it supports.
- The message hierarchy leads naturally to the actual offer and action.
