# Copywriting Originality And Claims Standard

## Status

- Classification: **Public repository policy**
- Applies to: copy, prompts, briefs, examples, templates, ads, emails, pages, scripts, and derived copywriting references
- Raw private swipe material: **Reference only; not approved for publication**
- Customer-facing publication: **Requires separate scoped approval**

## Ownership And Rights

A file being available to Sylvara does not establish copyright ownership, a redistribution license, customer consent, or permission to publish it. Sanitization and republication rights are separate gates.

Public copywriting artifacts must use one of these rights bases:

- **Sylvara original synthesis** - newly written guidance, structures, checklists, or synthetic examples;
- **Sylvara owned copy** - material created for Sylvara with confirmed ownership and no private data;
- **Licensed republication** - material covered by a documented license that permits the intended public use; or
- **Public domain** - material whose status has been verified for the relevant jurisdiction and version.

Unknown, mixed, or unverified rights permit private study only. They do not permit copying the source into GitHub, prompts, training data, examples, or customer deliverables.

## Transformative Use Rules

When learning from a private source:

1. identify the strategic decision being made;
2. describe the structure in original language;
3. separate the durable principle from the source's product, audience, claims, and voice;
4. create a new structure card or checklist without preserving distinctive expression;
5. apply it only to verified Sylvara or customer facts; and
6. compare the draft against the source for suspicious phrase, sequence, metaphor, rhythm, or naming similarity.

Do not perform name replacement, synonym replacement, sentence-level paraphrase, or close imitation. Do not ask an agent to write in the exact style of a living writer, brand, course, or proprietary source. Describe the desired attributes instead, such as direct, concise, evidence-led, conversational, technical, or premium.

## Claim Evidence

Every material claim must have an evidence state:

| Evidence state | Meaning | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified** | Current evidence supports the exact wording, scope, audience, and time period | May be proposed for publication after normal review |
| **Qualified** | Evidence supports a narrower statement | Use only the narrower approved wording |
| **Illustrative** | Clearly synthetic or hypothetical and not presented as a real result | Internal examples and marked mockups only |
| **Unknown** | Evidence is absent, stale, ambiguous, or inaccessible | Do not publish the claim |
| **Prohibited** | Misleading, fabricated, private, unlawfully discriminatory, or outside approved risk tolerance | Do not draft or publish |

The stronger or more specific the claim, the stronger and more specific the evidence must be. A source advertisement is evidence that someone used a claim, not evidence that the claim was true, compliant, effective, or appropriate for Sylvara.

## Testimonials, Proof, And Social Evidence

Never invent or materially rewrite:

- customers, testimonials, quotations, reviews, case studies, logos, awards, credentials, rankings, usage counts, or performance data;
- before-and-after outcomes, revenue, savings, time reductions, conversion rates, response rates, or return on investment;
- endorsements, partnerships, media coverage, certifications, approvals, or affiliations; or
- screenshots, conversations, recordings, analytics, or customer details.

Use only approved evidence with documented scope and consent. Preserve material limitations and context. Anonymous proof still requires an authentic source and approval; removing a name does not make fabricated or confidential evidence usable.

## Offers, Guarantees, Urgency, And Scarcity

- State exactly what is included, excluded, priced, billed, delivered, supported, and refundable.
- Do not manufacture deadlines, limited inventory, closing windows, waitlists, bonuses, capacity constraints, or price increases.
- A guarantee must match an approved operational policy that Sylvara can honor consistently.
- Make eligibility, conditions, time limits, exclusions, and the customer's required actions clear.
- Do not use risk reversal to hide meaningful risk, recurring billing, lock-in, data use, or cancellation friction.
- Avoid dark patterns, shame, coercion, fear exploitation, disguised ads, or intentionally confusing calls to action.

## High-Risk Domains

Financial, investing, tax, health, mental health, legal, employment, immigration, insurance, housing, credit, safety, and relationship claims require specialized review before publication. Do not infer compliance from a swipe file or historical advertisement.

For high-risk copy:

- identify the responsible reviewer;
- use current authoritative evidence;
- disclose material conditions and uncertainty;
- avoid guaranteed outcomes or individualized advice unless the approved service and reviewer support it; and
- stop when legal, regulatory, platform, consent, or evidence requirements are Unknown.

## Privacy And Confidentiality

Public copy and repository references must exclude customer or prospect PII, private emails, phone numbers, recordings, transcripts, screenshots, account details, analytics exports, private prompts, production identifiers, and confidential research.

Document metadata, image EXIF, PDF properties, comments, tracked changes, embedded files, links, and filenames can disclose private information even when the visible page appears safe. Raw Word, PDF, image, audio, video, email, and export files therefore remain outside this public repository unless separately cleared and sanitized.

## Brand And Audience Integrity

- Use the current approved product name, offer, vocabulary, and voice.
- Do not imply capabilities, integrations, availability, pricing, security, compliance, or support that has not been verified.
- Do not exploit protected characteristics, personal hardship, fear, shame, or sensitive data.
- Do not present aggressive direct-response conventions as the default Sylvara voice.
- Prefer a credible, specific reason to believe over exaggerated certainty.

## AI Drafting Rules

Before drafting, an agent must receive a completed brief or state which critical fields are Unknown. The agent must not use repository patterns as facts.

An AI-generated draft must:

- label assumptions and placeholder facts;
- keep unsupported claims out of customer-ready copy;
- create distinct strategic alternatives instead of superficial rewrites;
- preserve the primary action and channel constraints;
- pass originality, privacy, evidence, voice, and compliance review; and
- remain a proposal until a human approves publication.

## Review And Readback

Before publication:

1. compare every claim with the approved evidence ledger;
2. verify offer terms, price, dates, availability, and links against the authoritative system;
3. confirm consent and rights for every testimonial, logo, image, quotation, and attributed fact;
4. run the originality review against any private inspiration used;
5. remove private metadata and non-public identifiers;
6. obtain the required business, brand, legal, platform, or customer approval; and
7. read back the published page, message, or campaign to confirm the approved copy is what went live.

Repository merge approval is not authorization to send an email, launch an advertisement, publish a page, change a live prompt, or communicate with a customer.

## Validation

A governed copywriting artifact must state its purpose, audience, channel, owner, evidence source, claim status, rights basis, approval state, and rollback or correction path. If any required element is missing, the artifact remains a draft and fails closed for publication.
