# Copywriting Pattern Library

## Purpose

Provide original structure cards for common copywriting decisions. Patterns are thinking tools, not source copy, facts, approved claims, or fixed templates.

Every pattern requires a completed [copy brief](../templates/copy-brief.md), verified evidence, an appropriate channel, and review under the [Originality And Claims Standard](../originality-and-claims-standard.md).

## Pattern Metadata

Each pattern defines:

- **Use case:** the writing job it helps solve;
- **Channel:** where it commonly fits;
- **Funnel stage:** the reader decision stage;
- **Awareness:** one or more canonical stages: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or most-aware;
- **Risk:** the canonical review class: Low, Medium, or High;
- **Structure:** the sequence of ideas;
- **Required inputs:** facts needed before drafting; and
- **Failure modes:** ways the pattern becomes weak, misleading, or inappropriate.

## Positioning Patterns

### PAT-POS-01: Audience - Situation - Outcome

- **Use case:** establish immediate relevance.
- **Channel:** website, landing page, product page, email, or ad.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness through consideration.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware through most-aware.
- **Risk:** Low. Escalate to High when the outcome is regulated or quantitative.
- **Structure:** identify the audience in a recognizable situation; name the useful outcome; state the offer category or method.
- **Required inputs:** audience, triggering situation, outcome, offer category, evidence boundary.
- **Failure modes:** broad audience, universal outcome, category jargon, or no reason to believe.

### PAT-POS-02: Current Friction - Different Method - Operational Effect

- **Use case:** differentiate SaaS, automation, or a service workflow.
- **Channel:** product page, feature page, comparison page, sales page.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High when describing integrations, security, reliability, or savings.
- **Structure:** show current friction; explain the real method; connect it to a supportable operational effect.
- **Required inputs:** verified current alternative, method, capability, prerequisites, limitations.
- **Failure modes:** caricaturing alternatives, invented efficiency claims, or naming a mechanism with no substance.

### PAT-POS-03: Best Fit - Not Fit - Decision Rule

- **Use case:** improve trust and qualification.
- **Channel:** service page, pricing page, sales page, proposal, FAQ.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** state the situations where the offer fits; state material non-fit conditions; provide the next decision step.
- **Required inputs:** eligibility, exclusions, prerequisites, alternatives, buying path.
- **Failure modes:** fake disqualification designed only to create exclusivity, hidden exclusions, or an unclear alternative.

## Lead And Hook Patterns

### PAT-LEAD-01: Recognizable Moment

- **Use case:** open with a situation the reader immediately recognizes.
- **Channel:** email, landing page, social post, sales page.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** describe an observable moment; name the friction; transition to the underlying cause or opportunity.
- **Required inputs:** authentic situation, consequence, audience language.
- **Failure modes:** invented drama, emotional manipulation, or a generic scenario.

### PAT-LEAD-02: Assumption - Contradiction - Explanation

- **Use case:** reframe a familiar but weak approach.
- **Channel:** advertorial, email, webinar, long-form page, thought leadership.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness or consideration.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware or solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High when criticizing competitors or established guidance.
- **Structure:** state the common assumption fairly; show the verified limitation; explain the better decision rule.
- **Required inputs:** actual assumption, evidence, limitations, alternative.
- **Failure modes:** empty contrarianism, straw-man comparison, false dichotomy, or unsupported certainty.

### PAT-LEAD-03: Evidence - Meaning - Relevance

- **Use case:** lead with strong proof or a verified observation.
- **Channel:** case page, sales page, email, presentation, advertorial.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware or product-aware.
- **Risk:** High because evidence scope and consent matter.
- **Structure:** present the approved evidence; explain what it does and does not show; connect it to the reader's decision.
- **Required inputs:** evidence, source, consent, population, period, conditions, limitations.
- **Failure modes:** cherry-picking, causal overreach, fabricated precision, or irrelevant proof.

### PAT-LEAD-04: Diagnostic Question - Criteria - Next Step

- **Use case:** help a reader assess fit or priority.
- **Channel:** lead magnet, article, email, landing page, consultation page.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness or consideration.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware.
- **Risk:** High in medical, financial, legal, or other advice contexts.
- **Structure:** pose a relevant question; provide transparent criteria; explain what different results mean; offer the next appropriate step.
- **Required inputs:** valid criteria, limits, decision owner, next step.
- **Failure modes:** fake scoring, disguised diagnosis, fear-based qualification, or a predetermined result.

## Proof And Objection Patterns

### PAT-PROOF-01: Claim - Mechanism - Evidence - Limit

- **Use case:** support an important claim transparently.
- **Channel:** any persuasive asset.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** unaware through most-aware when the claim is relevant.
- **Risk:** Medium. Escalate to High for quantitative, comparative, or regulated claims.
- **Structure:** state the narrow claim; explain why it is plausible; provide matched evidence; disclose the material limit.
- **Required inputs:** approved claim, mechanism, evidence, limitation.
- **Failure modes:** proof by assertion, hidden conditions, or evidence that supports a different claim.

### PAT-PROOF-02: Capability - Workflow - Operational Consequence

- **Use case:** make product or service proof concrete without inventing outcomes.
- **Channel:** SaaS, service, feature, and technical pages.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High for security, integration, performance, and availability claims.
- **Structure:** show the verified capability; place it in the customer's workflow; state the supportable operational consequence.
- **Required inputs:** live capability, prerequisites, workflow, limits.
- **Failure modes:** planned capability presented as live, generic benefit language, or unjustified financial impact.

### PAT-PROOF-03: Situation - Action - Verified Result - Boundary

- **Use case:** present an approved case or testimonial.
- **Channel:** case study, page, email, sales asset.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** High due to consent, representativeness, and result claims.
- **Structure:** define the starting situation; explain the relevant action; state the verified result; clarify conditions and non-guarantee.
- **Required inputs:** authentic source, consent, dates, actions, result, limitations.
- **Failure modes:** composite customer, invented quotation, omitted conditions, or atypical result presented as expected.

### PAT-OBJ-01: Concern - Why It Is Reasonable - Factual Resolution

- **Use case:** answer an objection without pressure.
- **Channel:** email, FAQ, landing page, sales page, proposal.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** name the concern accurately; acknowledge why it matters; answer with process, evidence, scope, or terms; provide the next step.
- **Required inputs:** real objection, verified resolution, limits, owner.
- **Failure modes:** dismissing the reader, inventing certainty, or answering a different objection.

## Offer Patterns

### PAT-OFFER-01: Core Outcome - Deliverables - Process - Terms

- **Use case:** make an offer understandable.
- **Channel:** service page, sales page, proposal, pricing page, email.
- **Funnel stage:** decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware or most-aware.
- **Risk:** High when price, renewal, or service scope is complex.
- **Structure:** state the intended outcome; list essential deliverables; explain delivery; state price and material terms.
- **Required inputs:** current scope, timeline, responsibilities, price, billing, support, exclusions.
- **Failure modes:** vague deliverables, hidden recurring terms, or outcome treated as a guarantee.

### PAT-OFFER-02: Core Offer - Adoption Support - Confidence Support

- **Use case:** build a coherent offer stack.
- **Channel:** sales page, proposal, checkout, product page.
- **Funnel stage:** decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** present the core offer; add what helps implementation; add what reduces legitimate risk or uncertainty.
- **Required inputs:** real components, support capacity, cost, conditions.
- **Failure modes:** unrelated bonuses, inflated value arithmetic, support commitments the team cannot meet.

### PAT-OFFER-03: Pilot - Success Criteria - Expansion Decision

- **Use case:** reduce risk for a complex or high-consideration service.
- **Channel:** proposal, service page, sales conversation follow-up.
- **Funnel stage:** decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** High when success criteria affect payment or legal obligations.
- **Structure:** define pilot scope; state responsibilities and evidence; define success and stop criteria; explain the expansion decision.
- **Required inputs:** scope, owner, timeline, measures, rollback, price, approval.
- **Failure modes:** vague pilot, free work with no decision path, or metrics outside the offer's control.

### PAT-OFFER-04: Real Constraint - Verified Deadline - Consequence

- **Use case:** communicate legitimate urgency or scarcity.
- **Channel:** email, landing page, checkout, event page.
- **Funnel stage:** decision.
- **Awareness:** most-aware.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Structure:** name the real constraint; state the exact deadline or capacity; explain what changes afterward; provide the action.
- **Required inputs:** authoritative constraint, current date or quantity, owner, post-deadline state.
- **Failure modes:** evergreen countdown, invented capacity, repeated final notice, or hidden terms.

## Page Patterns

### PAT-PAGE-01: Focused Landing Page

- **Use case:** convert one traffic source toward one action.
- **Channel:** landing page.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness through decision.
- **Awareness:** use the brief's selected canonical awareness stage.
- **Risk:** Low. Escalate to High based on the claims used.
- **Structure:** message match; outcome; problem or context; mechanism; proof; offer; objections; action; next-step expectation.
- **Required inputs:** traffic message, audience, offer, proof, CTA, form needs.
- **Failure modes:** multiple audiences, multiple actions, mismatch with source, or excessive form friction.

### PAT-PAGE-02: Long-Form Decision Page

- **Use case:** support an unfamiliar, complex, or high-consideration decision.
- **Channel:** long-form sales page.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness through decision.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware through most-aware.
- **Risk:** High due to the volume of claims and proof.
- **Structure:** lead; problem; insight or mechanism; solution; proof; offer; objections; terms; action; recap.
- **Required inputs:** full brief, evidence ledger, offer, approvals.
- **Failure modes:** repetition, unresolved open loops, buried terms, or length without decision value.

### PAT-PAGE-03: SaaS Product Page

- **Use case:** explain product fit and workflow.
- **Channel:** product or feature page.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High for integration, performance, privacy, and security claims.
- **Structure:** job and audience; workflow; capabilities; operational effects; proof; integrations and requirements; onboarding; action.
- **Required inputs:** live product state, metadata, supported integrations, limitations, proof.
- **Failure modes:** feature dump, planned features presented as available, or no use-case hierarchy.

### PAT-PAGE-04: Fair Comparison

- **Use case:** help a buyer choose among approaches or plans.
- **Channel:** comparison, alternatives, or pricing page.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High when naming competitors.
- **Structure:** decision criteria; current dated facts; meaningful differences; fit and non-fit; next step.
- **Required inputs:** verified comparison date, sources, legal review when required.
- **Failure modes:** stale facts, unfair criteria, hidden weaknesses, or unsupported superiority.

## Email Patterns

### PAT-EMAIL-01: Welcome And Orientation

- **Use case:** fulfill an opt-in or onboarding expectation.
- **Channel:** email.
- **Funnel stage:** post-conversion or onboarding.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** confirm why the message arrived; deliver expected value; orient the reader; give one next step; set future expectations.
- **Required inputs:** consent source, promised asset, next step, preference path.
- **Failure modes:** immediate unrelated pitch, missing promised value, or unclear sender.

### PAT-EMAIL-02: Insight To Application

- **Use case:** educate while moving toward a relevant next step.
- **Channel:** email or nurture sequence.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness or consideration.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** recognizable situation; useful insight; practical application; optional next step.
- **Required inputs:** accurate insight, audience situation, application, CTA.
- **Failure modes:** vague inspiration, hidden sales turn, or teaching disconnected from the offer.

### PAT-EMAIL-03: Proof And Relevance

- **Use case:** use approved evidence to reduce uncertainty.
- **Channel:** email.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Structure:** decision question; approved evidence; limitation; relevance; action.
- **Required inputs:** proof, consent, scope, reader context.
- **Failure modes:** invented story, decontextualized result, or implied guarantee.

### PAT-EMAIL-04: One Objection

- **Use case:** resolve one material concern.
- **Channel:** email.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** product-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** state concern; acknowledge stakes; answer with fact, process, or term; invite the next step.
- **Required inputs:** actual objection, verified answer, CTA.
- **Failure modes:** defensive tone, false reassurance, or addressing several objections superficially.

### PAT-EMAIL-05: Respectful Reactivation

- **Use case:** re-engage an inactive subscriber or customer.
- **Channel:** email.
- **Funnel stage:** reactivation.
- **Awareness:** use the brief's selected canonical awareness stage.
- **Risk:** High for consent and frequency.
- **Structure:** acknowledge time or changed context; offer fresh relevance; provide a useful action, preference option, or clean exit.
- **Required inputs:** lawful contact basis, current relationship, preference controls, relevant value.
- **Failure modes:** fake reply, guilt, manufactured emergency, or hiding unsubscribe.

## Advertising Patterns

### PAT-AD-01: Situation - Outcome - Next Step

- **Use case:** concise paid-social or display concept.
- **Channel:** paid social or display.
- **Funnel stage:** awareness.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware.
- **Risk:** Low. Escalate to High based on targeting and claims.
- **Structure:** cue a recognizable situation; state a supportable outcome; give one next step.
- **Required inputs:** audience, approved targeting, outcome, landing page, CTA.
- **Failure modes:** personal-attribute inference, broad promise, or landing-page mismatch.

### PAT-AD-02: Mechanism Demonstration

- **Use case:** show how a product or workflow works.
- **Channel:** video, social, display, or product ad.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High for product accuracy.
- **Structure:** show the current task; demonstrate the verified mechanism; show the immediate operational effect; invite exploration.
- **Required inputs:** live product, accurate visual, prerequisites, limits.
- **Failure modes:** simulated capability presented as live or an outcome not shown by the demonstration.

### PAT-AD-03: Intent Match

- **Use case:** search advertisement.
- **Channel:** paid search.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration or decision.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware or product-aware.
- **Risk:** Low.
- **Structure:** match task or category; state meaningful qualification or distinction; provide the closest next step.
- **Required inputs:** query intent, landing page, verified differentiator, CTA.
- **Failure modes:** generic destination, irrelevant keyword insertion, or misleading qualification.

### PAT-AD-04: Evidence-Led Angle

- **Use case:** lead with a verified result, demonstration, or fact.
- **Channel:** paid social, search, or display.
- **Funnel stage:** consideration.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Structure:** approved evidence; narrow meaning; audience relevance; action.
- **Required inputs:** evidence, consent, conditions, landing-page support.
- **Failure modes:** missing context, typicality implication, fabricated visual, or unsupported extrapolation.

## Pattern Selection Checklist

- The pattern fits the audience's awareness and decision stage.
- The channel can support the required explanation and proof.
- Required inputs are verified or clearly marked Unknown.
- The risk class has the appropriate reviewer.
- The pattern improves strategic clarity rather than forcing the source's sequence.
- The final draft uses original language and current brand voice.
- The call to action matches reader readiness and actual offer state.

## Synthetic Family Examples

These examples are fictional, deliberately generic, and provided only to demonstrate structure. They are not Sylvara capabilities, customer outcomes, approved claims, or publishable copy.

- **Positioning:** For an operations lead whose requests are scattered across inboxes, a fictional intake assistant organizes each request into one review queue through a clearly defined routing workflow.
- **Lead:** When the same request appears in three channels, the visible problem is repetition; the deeper issue may be that no system owns intake.
- **Proof and objection:** The fictional workflow records a review state before any downstream action. That supports an auditability claim, but not a speed, savings, or accuracy claim without separate evidence.
- **Offer:** The fictional pilot includes one workflow, an agreed test fixture, a review period, and a documented stop-or-expand decision; it does not imply guaranteed results.
- **Page:** Start with the audience's operational situation, explain the bounded method, show approved evidence and limitations, state the exact offer, then present one proportionate next step.
- **Email:** Orient the reader to one useful idea, explain why it matters in their current situation, give one applicable step, and invite one relevant action.
- **Advertisement:** Name a recognizable task, state one verified distinction, and route the reader to a landing page that supports the same promise and qualification.
